Crop Break-Even Price and Yield: A Farm Budget Guide

Use crop break-even price and yield formulas to understand the yield or crop price needed to cover a production budget.

A crop break-even calculation links yield, sale price, and production cost. It can help frame a budget discussion, test a crop-price scenario, or compare how a change in yield affects the return after entered costs. It is only meaningful when yield units, price units, acreage, and cost categories match.

Calculate break-even price

Break-even price per unit equals total cost per acre divided by expected yield per acre. If total production cost is $650 per acre and expected yield is 190 bushels per acre, the break-even sale price is about $3.42 per bushel. This simple result changes if the yield, costs, marketing deductions, storage, drying, basis, or delivered price changes.

Calculate break-even yield

Break-even yield per acre equals total cost per acre divided by sale price per unit. At $650 per acre and $4.50 per bushel, break-even yield is about 144.4 bu/acre. The formula assumes each unit of output receives the entered sale price. It does not predict what the field will yield or guarantee that the market will pay the input price.

Choose the cost basis before comparing crops

Include the costs that match the question. A variable-cost break-even can be useful for a short-term decision, while a full cost-of-production estimate may also include land, labor, machinery depreciation, interest, repairs, storage, insurance, taxes, and marketing. Make sure to avoid double-counting machinery or custom work if it is already included in another line.

Test more than one scenario

Farm economics change with yield and crop price. Run the calculator with a conservative yield, a likely yield, and a stronger yield. If the farm faces variable input or harvest costs, change costs too rather than assuming all expenses remain constant. A sensitivity tool can show how margin changes under more than one scenario, but it does not assign probabilities to prices or yields.

Keep estimates separate from decisions

The Crop Break-Even Price and Yield Calculator shows the arithmetic using your entries. A full budget should use current local prices, rent terms, crop insurance and program assumptions where relevant, and complete costs. Iowa State University Extension’s Ag Decision Maker tools are a useful reference for farm budgets and decision analysis. For major financing, tax, or lease decisions, consider qualified professional advice.

This guide is educational and describes a calculation method. For local crop rates or operating decisions, use your state Extension service, current product labels, supplier records, and the sources listed on our research and sources page.
