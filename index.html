Questions and answers

FAQs About Agriculture Calculators

Quick answers about farm calculation formulas, units, and estimates.

What agriculture calculators can I use on this website?

Agriculture Calculator has tools for seed cost and rate, plant population, fertilizer and nutrient budgeting, crop yield and revenue, sprayer flow, irrigation water, grain moisture shrink, harvest, farm finance, machinery, livestock, and unit conversion.

Is there a seed cost per acre calculator for US farms?

Yes. The Seed Cost Per Acre Calculator estimates seed pounds and total cost using acreage, a seeding rate in pounds per acre, and seed price per pound. Use the seed population tool when germination and target stand are part of your calculation.

How do I calculate fertilizer cost per acre?

Multiply pounds of fertilizer product applied per acre by the product price per pound. When the price is quoted per US short ton, divide the per-acre product pounds by 2,000 and multiply by the price per ton. This cost estimate does not choose a nutrient rate.

Can these calculators estimate sprayer nozzle flow and gallons per acre?

Yes. The nozzle-flow calculator estimates required gallons per minute from target gallons per acre, travel speed, and nozzle spacing. Compare the result with a real calibration test and follow the pesticide label.

How is grain moisture shrink calculated?

The grain moisture calculator uses a dry-matter balance: final weight equals incoming weight multiplied by (100 minus current moisture) divided by (100 minus target moisture). Commercial shrink schedules and drying charges may be different.

Are these agriculture calculators free and private?

The calculator tools are free. Their calculations run in your browser and calculator entries are not sent to our server. The contact form uses a third-party form processor, and normal website hosting may process request logs.

Are calculator results official agronomic recommendations?

No. Results are estimates based on the inputs and formulas shown on each page. Use a current soil test, product labels, equipment manuals, state Extension recommendations, and qualified professional advice when a decision requires them.

Use reliable field data

A calculator is only as useful as its inputs. Check product labels, seed tags, soil tests, supplier quotes, measured equipment output, grain tickets, and local Extension recommendations before using a result for an operational decision.
